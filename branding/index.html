How MarineSensitivity apps look, and what each control means

This page applies the Marine Minerals Administration Branding Guidelines (July 2026) to interactive software: the Marine Sensitivity Atlas first, and any app that follows it.

The bureau's guide is the authority on the seal, colours, typefaces and the two graphic elements. It does not cover screens, themes, data colour or controls. Those are set here, and each rule on this page is held in the Atlas by a token, a component or a test.

Identity and seal

The agency name is spelled Marine Minerals Administration, as on the seal, with U.S. Department of the Interior as the second line of a lockup.

The seal is used unmodified. The guide's rules, and how the Atlas meets them:

  • Minimum size: 0.75 in wide in print; at least 72 CSS px on screen.
  • Clear space: the height of the offshore platform in the seal, on all four sides. On screen that is a quarter of the seal's height.
  • Background: a plain one. The Atlas puts it on a white plate in both themes, never on a glass panel or over the map.
  • Versions: full colour by default; one colour in a dark colour on a plain ground, sparingly; black and white rarely.

Where the seal appears in the Atlas

  • the About dialog, with the full lockup;
  • the header of a printed report;
  • the on-map "About this release" card, which is opaque and collapsed by default.

It never appears in the 48 px top bar, which cannot hold a 72 px seal. The top bar carries the project's own mark instead.

The dashed line marks the clear space. Nothing else enters it.

The project mark is a wave in a hexagon, the seal's two graphic elements in one shape. It is the app icon and the top-bar mark. Its ring is Gold on the dark theme and Navy on the light theme.

Do

  • Give the image both width and height so it cannot distort.
  • Load it lazily, from one source file.
  • Pair it with the agency line when there is room.

Do not

  • Stretch, skew or squeeze it.
  • Add shadows or gradients.
  • Use greyscale, or a low-resolution copy.
  • Crop or remove any part.
  • Crowd it against another seal.

Typography

Century Gothic, Bold for headlines, never lighter than Regular. Geometric, all-caps for section titles, as in the guide.

Hexagon and wave

The guide takes two elements from the seal and makes them secondary graphics: the hexagon pattern, in Steel Blue or Gold, and the wave, in Steel Blue and Navy. Both are used at 10% opacity, as texture that does not compete with content.

In the app they are masks over one tint token, Gold on the dark theme and Steel on the light theme, so they can never carry a stray colour. A test fails if the opacity is raised above 10%.

  • Hexagon: panel-header texture, empty states, the loading honeycomb, the report cover band. Also a shape: the project mark and category swatches.
  • Wave: the top bar's lower edge, the phone sheet's upper edge, dialog dividers, report header and footer.
  • Neither sits behind more than one line of body text.

Spacing, shape, motion

Spacing

4812162432

Six steps. Fixed sizes: top bar 48, spine 56 to 64, side panel 380 (resizable 320 to 720), minimum touch target 44, seal minimum 72.

Shape

Card 12, control 8, pill 999. Three shadow levels: resting chrome, floating cards, overlays. The shadow colour is a token; black shadows belong to dark themes only.

Motion

150 ms for a control changing state, 200 ms for a panel or sheet, one ease-out curve.

Both become 0 ms when the reader asks for reduced motion. The loader is seven hexagons pulsing in turn; with reduced motion it is a still honeycomb and the status text.

Control grammar

One look, one job. A look never stands for two different things.

Adopted 2026-09-30, after the same gold pill had come to mean "change the data" in one place and "change what this pane shows" in another. Atlas 0.10.81 to 0.10.83, in build

Switch

Changes the data. Afterwards the map, the legend and the table all show different numbers.

  • Scores | Species
  • Raster cells | Program areas
  • A species' model input
  • Original | Interpolated

A pill. Gold fill and Navy label on the selected segment. Two to four options, one always selected. Segmented.svelte, role="radiogroup".

Tabs

Changes the view of the same data, inside one surface. The content under the line swaps; the selection does not.

  • Table: Species · Zones · Composition

Plain labels on a hairline. The selected one is bold with a 3 px underline: Gold on the dark theme, Navy on the light. Tabs.svelte, role="tablist", arrow keys move.

Spine

Changes the surface. Each entry opens a different working surface.

  • Layers · Details · Table · Report
  • Same four, same order, both lenses, every screen size

Icon over label. Gold fill and Navy label on the current entry, aria-current. Rail.svelte.

How to choose

  • Would the map, the legend and the table all show different numbers afterwards? It is a switch.
  • Is the same selection only being shown another way, in the same pane? It is tabs.
  • Does it open a different place to work? It is a spine entry.
  • None of these? It is a button, a select or a checkbox. There is no fourth toggle style.

Do

  • Keep tabs to one level.
  • Name a surface for what the reader gets there: "Details", not the name of one chart.
  • Use a select once a switch would need more than four options.

Do not

  • Style tabs as a pill, or a switch as an underline. The shape is the meaning.
  • Put tabs inside tabs. One level is really a spine entry or a switch.
  • Give one lens its own spine entry. If a surface only fits one lens, rename it until it fits both.

Layout

The map is the page. Everything else floats over it as cards. There is no fixed sidebar.

  • The spine is attached to the panel it controls, on the panel's outer edge, and moves with it. The pair docks left by default; one button moves it to the other side.
  • Selecting the current entry again collapses the panel. The spine stays. The header needs only "move to the other side" and "collapse".
  • A surface takes the width its content needs. Layers, Details and Report open as a side panel. Table takes the whole stage, with a "Map" button to return.
  • The panel header says what is being shown ("Score · Raster cells", "Cell 3058375"), not the name of the spine entry beside it.
  • The legend sits on the opposite side from the panel, and every camera move allows for the panel, the legend and the phone sheet.
  • On a phone (under 900 px) the spine is the bottom bar and the panel is a sheet above it, with peek, half and full heights. Table opens the sheet at full height.

Components

Every component lives in atlas/src/lib/ui/ and is shown in both themes at three widths in the Atlas gallery, which CI compares against saved screenshots. States that mean something are also in the accessibility tree, never in colour alone.

Button

One primary (Gold) button per surface, for the action the surface exists for. Everything else is a plain button. A disabled button says why in a tooltip.

Click, inspect, tabulate, report

  1. Click a cell or a Program Area. It is outlined in pink and becomes the "last clicked" place.
  2. Details shows its breakdown: the flower plot in Scores, the species information in Species.
  3. Table lists the species for it, at full width.
  4. Report keeps an explicit list of places. A new click replaces only the last-clicked slot, never a place the reader added.

Each surface says in one sentence what it is showing, for example "Reporting on the last clicked place. Add it to keep it."

Report is one flow

Places at the top, ways to add a place below, and the primary "Open report" button pinned at the bottom with the sentence that says what it will report on. No sub-tabs. in build

The link is the view

Everything a shared link should reproduce lives in the URL: release, lens, layer, selection, places and the arrangement of the panel. A control whose state matters to a colleague must write it there.

Icons

Icons come from Material Design Icons, 24 px grid, one colour (the text colour of their control), and are imported by name. Path data is never typed by hand: the first attempt from memory got none of sixteen right.

One glyph is bespoke. The flower is a circle with eight petals of clearly different lengths, because that is what the flower plot draws: the composite in the centre and one petal per component.

The full name-to-icon map is in the Atlas design spec, section 6.

Accessibility

The bureau's guide requires every product to meet or exceed Section 508. For these apps that means WCAG 2.1 AA, checked by tools on every build and by hand on every release.

Words

  • One name per thing, everywhere. A Program Area's name is resolved by one function and appears identically in search, tooltip, flower title, table header, report and download.
  • Say what is being shown. "Species in GOA Program Area A (GAA)", "Reporting on 2 places", "Raster cells · whole layer".
  • Say why something is unavailable, in the control's tooltip, in plain words.
  • Absence is stated, not implied. A species card shows both statutes every time: "MMPA · floor 20" for a marine mammal, "MBTA · not applicable" for a turtle.
  • Sentence case for labels and buttons. All-caps is for section titles in the display face only.
  • Scientific names in italics; common names as published.
